# What the field does at collapsed repeats

Taken 2026-11-23 by a Fable reviewer with web access, for the owner's
question whether indexing a graph by reference position is a broken idea.
The full text, with its tables of builders, viewers, figures and a source
list marked by how much of each was read, is
`~/src/walks8-evidence/sessions-20261122/field-review/review.txt`. It read
captions, methods, documentation and source, not figure images.

## Checked a second time, from a separate fetch

- **Li, Feng and Chu 2020, the paper that defines rGFA** (arXiv:2003.06079
  v1; the journal wording was not compared): "In rGFA, each segment is
  associated with one origin", which "forbids the collapse of different
  regions from one sequence". And in its discussion: "In a collapsed graph,
  the definition of orthology is not clear because multiple sequences from
  the same sample may go through the same segment. Without the concept of
  orthology, we cannot define variations, either." And: "In our view,
  however, the reference pangenome graph should not be a collapsed graph."
- **VRPG's README**, a viewer that places a graph on a reference
  coordinate: "For graphs generated by PGGB, the duplicate sequence in the
  primary linear reference assembly might have been collapsed. VRPG will
  re-insert shadow nodes and edges into the graph" to "restore the
  linearity", and those nodes "can be recognized by the fact that no
  highlighted path goes through them". It copies nodes for the reference
  only and assigns no haplotype step to a copy.

## From the review, not checked again

- **Which builders let a reference path pass a node twice:** pggb by
  design, progressive Cactus through hal2vg, and Minigraph-Cactus with
  `--collapse`. Not minigraph, not default Minigraph-Cactus for its first
  reference, not a graph built from a VCF.
- **Which graph the public pangenomes report from:** HPRC release 2 says it
  reports the GRCh38 Minigraph-Cactus results unless noted. The Chinese and
  Arab pangenomes are Minigraph-Cactus. The 2023 cattle set includes
  progressive Cactus.
- **"Which copy" is never defined except by a stated rule:** `odgi
  untangle` takes the best base-weighted Jaccard and reports a score, a
  self-coverage and an n-th best; `vg deconstruct` assigns by similarity of
  flanking context and reports no score; halSynteny and the UCSC tracks
  take chains. The review found no evaluation of any such rule.
- **Published figures of hard loci** (amylase, TSPY and DAZ, the Y
  palindromes, RCCX, TBC1D3) draw one row per haplotype in its own
  coordinates, coloured by shared sequence or by gene, often straight from
  the assemblies. None draws a per-copy match to a reference as a fact of
  a graph. The nearest are untangle plots, which state a best match.
- **What graphs are credited with at such loci:** structural haplotype
  classes and copy numbers. **What their authors disclaim:** minigraph's
  README says not to overinterpret complex subgraphs, and the
  Minigraph-Cactus paper says it cannot satisfactorily align highly
  repetitive sequence.
- **Tool runs that would settle more,** with commands, are at the end of
  the review: `odgi untangle` and `vg deconstruct -u` on the TSPY window of
  pggb chrY, and VRPG's `gfa2view`. None was run. odgi 0.9.0 and vg are on
  ada.
